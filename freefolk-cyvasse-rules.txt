FREEFOLK - CYVASSE
Rules of Play
==========================================================================


OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two players, Ivory and Onyx, each build their own homeland from terrain
squares, deploy an army of 16 pieces, and then fight to capture the
enemy King.

The board is an 8 x 8 frame. Ivory's homeland is rows 1-4 and Onyx's
homeland is rows 5-8. A gold line marks the border between them.


==========================================================================
1. SETUP
==========================================================================

The game begins with an empty frame: no squares and no pieces.

Step 1 - Placing squares
  - Ivory places all 32 of its squares, then Onyx places its 32.
  - Squares may only be placed in your own homeland.
  - Choose a square type in the tray, then click an empty spot.
  - To rearrange, click one of your placed squares to pick it up, then
    click an empty spot to move it, or another of your squares to swap
    the two. Click the same square again to put it down.
  - When all 32 are placed, press "Done".

Step 2 - Placing pieces
  - Ivory places all 16 of its pieces, then Onyx places its 16.
  - Pieces may only be placed in the first 3 rows of your homeland
    (Ivory: rows 1-3, Onyx: rows 6-8). Your 4th homeland row starts empty.
  - Choose a piece in the tray, then click an empty square.
  - Placed pieces can be moved or swapped the same way as squares.
  - When all 16 are placed, press "Done".

Secrecy
  - While a player is setting up, the other player's squares and pieces
    are hidden.
  - After each "Done" the board turns to face the next player and a
    5-second countdown runs with everything hidden, so the other player
    can look away.
  - When both armies are placed, the whole board is revealed and Ivory
    moves first.


==========================================================================
2. SQUARES (TERRAIN)
==========================================================================

Each player has 32 squares:

  Square     Count   Looks like       Cost to enter
  --------   -----   --------------   ------------------------------
  Forest       12    Jadeite          2 movement
  Mountain      6    Carnelian        All remaining movement
  Stream       12    Lapis lazuli     1 movement
  Fortress      2    Slate            1 movement

Forest (green jadeite)
  - Costs 2 movement to enter.
  - A Heavy horse standing on a Forest cannot be captured (except by an
    Assassin).

Mountain (orange-red carnelian)
  - Entering uses up all of a piece's remaining movement, so the move
    ends there. A piece needs at least 1 movement left to enter.

Stream (blue lapis lazuli)
  - Ordinary ground. Costs 1 movement.

Fortress (grey slate)
  - Costs 1 movement to enter.
  - A piece standing on an intact Fortress cannot be captured by a normal
    attack. Instead the attack RUINS the Fortress:
      * the defending piece stays on the board, on the ruined square;
      * the attacking piece stops on the closest empty square along its
        attack path (if it started right next to the Fortress, it stays
        where it was);
      * the turn ends.
  - A ruined Fortress is marked with a brush-painted red X. It behaves
    like an ordinary square and gives no more protection.
  - Exceptions: a Trebuchet or Catapult ruins the Fortress AND captures
    the piece. An Assassin ignores Fortresses.
  - This protects whichever piece is standing on the Fortress, Ivory or
    Onyx.

Some pieces have their own terrain rules; see Section 4.


==========================================================================
3. GENERAL RULES OF PLAY
==========================================================================

Turns
  - Players alternate. On your turn you move exactly one piece.

Movement
  - Pieces move in a straight line along a row or column (never
    diagonally), unless their own rules say otherwise.
  - Each piece has a number of movement points per turn (see the table
    in Section 4). Each square entered costs movement according to its
    terrain (Section 2).
  - Pieces cannot pass through or jump over other pieces, unless their
    own rules say otherwise.

Control
  - A piece controls the square it stands on.

Capturing
  - A piece may capture the first enemy piece in its path if it has
    enough movement to enter that square.
  - A piece may only capture an enemy of EQUAL OR LOWER power.
  - Any piece may capture a King.
  - If the attacker and defender have EQUAL power, both are captured.
  - Some pieces have special capture rules; see Section 4.

Hover over any piece or square to see its name, power, movement and
the terrain it stands on.


==========================================================================
4. THE PIECES
==========================================================================

Each player has 16 pieces:

  Piece         Count   Power   Movement
  -----------   -----   -----   --------
  King            1      10        1
  Dragon          1       9        7
  Elephant        2       8        6
  Heavy horse     1       7        5
  Trebuchet       1       6        5
  Light horse     1       5        4
  Catapult        1       4        4
  Crossbow        2       3        3
  Spear           2       2        2
  Rabble          4       1        1  (2 when Advancing)


KING  (Power 10, Movement 1)
  - Cannot leave its homeland. It cannot capture a piece standing across
    the border either.
  - Can be captured by ANY piece.
  - Capturing the enemy King wins the game. If two Kings capture each
    other (equal power), both fall and the game is a draw.

DRAGON  (Power 9, Movement 7)
  - Ignores terrain: every square costs it 1 movement, including Forests
    and Mountains.
  - Can be captured by: the King, another Dragon (both are captured),
    a Trebuchet, a Catapult, a Crossbow firing from high ground (see
    Crossbow), or an Assassin.

ELEPHANT  (Power 8, Movement 6)
  - Climbing a Mountain costs it 2 movement instead of all remaining, so
    it can keep moving afterwards.
  - CHARGE: if the Elephant spends exactly all 6 movement crossing empty
    squares, it may capture the piece on the very next square in that
    line (equal or lower power, or a King). The Elephant ends on its
    last square and the target is removed. To charge, click the piece
    being captured. A charge into an intact Fortress ruins it instead.

HEAVY HORSE  (Power 7, Movement 5)
  - While standing on a Forest it cannot be captured, except by an
    Assassin.
  - Cannot capture Spears.

TREBUCHET  (Power 6, Movement 5)
  - Cannot enter Mountain squares.
  - Capturing: it may jump over any number of pieces in its line,
    friendly or enemy, and ignores terrain costs, as long as the target
    is within 5 squares. It cannot capture a piece on a Mountain.
  - Can capture a Dragon.
  - Attacking a piece on an intact Fortress ruins the Fortress AND
    captures the piece.
  - Moving without capturing follows the normal rules.

LIGHT HORSE  (Power 5, Movement 4)
  - Mountains cost it only 1 movement.
  - May make ONE 90-degree turn at any point during its move, and must
    finish the move in the new direction (for example, 2 squares east
    then 2 squares north). It cannot turn back the way it came.
  - Cannot capture Spears.

CATAPULT  (Power 4, Movement 4)
  - Capturing: it may jump over any number of pieces in its line,
    friendly or enemy, and ignores terrain costs, as long as the target
    is within 4 squares. Unlike the Trebuchet, it can capture a piece on
    a Mountain.
  - Can capture a Dragon.
  - Attacking a piece on an intact Fortress ruins the Fortress AND
    captures the piece.
  - Moving without capturing follows the normal rules.

CROSSBOW  (Power 3, Movement 3)
  - Can capture a Dragon if it STARTS its move on a Mountain or an
    intact Fortress. It loses this ability on a ruined Fortress.

SPEAR  (Power 2, Movement 2)
  - Can capture a Light horse or a Heavy horse, despite their higher
    power. A Heavy horse on a Forest is still protected.

RABBLE  (Power 1, Movement 1)
  - Forest: a Rabble can always enter a Forest, as if it had 2 movement.
    The move ends there.
  - ADVANCING: a Rabble has 2 movement this turn if
      * another friendly Rabble is on an adjacent square (including
        diagonally), or
      * it starts its turn outside its own homeland.
    A Rabble whose first step leaves its homeland may also take a second
    step. An Advancing Rabble pays the normal 2 movement to enter a
    Forest.
  - ASSASSIN: when a Rabble reaches the far edge of the opponent's
    homeland (row 8 for Ivory, row 1 for Onyx), it becomes an Assassin.
    Its player immediately chooses one enemy piece standing anywhere in
    their OWN homeland and captures it. The Rabble is then captured by
    the opponent and the turn passes.
      * The enemy King cannot be targeted unless it is that player's
        last piece on the board. Then it can be targeted anywhere, in
        either homeland.
      * Assassins ignore Fortress and Forest protection.
      * If there is no valid target, the Rabble is still lost.


==========================================================================
5. ENDING THE GAME
==========================================================================

King captured
  - Capturing the enemy King wins: "King captured - Ivory wins" (or
    Onyx wins).

Both kings fell
  - If both Kings are captured at the same time: "Both kings fell -
    Draw".

The Gods have won
  - If a player is left with only their King, a counter appears in the
    status bar. If that lone King survives five of its own turns, the
    game ends: "The Gods have won - Draw".

"Death in two"
  - This warning appears in the status bar, and the King's square glows
    red, when the player to move has a King the opponent could capture
    on their next turn.


==========================================================================
6. CONTROLS
==========================================================================

  - Click a piece to select it. Bright green dots show where it can
    move; bright red-orange rings mark pieces it can capture. Click a
    marked square to move.
  - Drag to orbit the camera. Scroll or pinch to zoom.
  - New game: start over.
  - Undo: take back the last action, including setup actions. It is
    blocked during the handover countdown.
  - Flip view: turn the board around.
  - Auto-turn: turn the board toward the player whose move it is.
